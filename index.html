Breathings, Accents, Iota Subscript & Punctuation
Essential reading rules in OCR GCSE Classical Greek (every initial vowel or diphthong carries a breathing mark).

1. Smooth vs. Rough Breathings (῾ / ᾿)

• Smooth Breathing ( ᾿ ): Curved like a closing quote (like ἀ, ἐ, ὀ). Pronounced with no h- sound: ἀγαθός (agathos), ἐν (en), ὄνομα (onoma).
• Rough Breathing ( ῾ ): Curved like an opening quote (like ἁ, ἑ, ὁ, ἱ, ὑ, ῥ). Always adds an initial h- sound: ὁ (ho), ἡμέρα (hēmera), ἵππος (hippos → Eng. hippo), ὑπέρ (huper → hyper).
• Diphthongs: Breathing sits on the 2nd vowel: αὐτός (autos, smooth), οἱ (hoi, rough), εὑρίσκω (heuriskō, rough).
• Mandatory Rough Breathings: Initial upsilon (ὑ-, e.g. ὕδωρ) and initial rho (ῥ-, e.g. ῥήτωρ rhētōr) always take a rough breathing.
• Capital Letters: Breathing appears in front of the capital letter: Ἀθῆναι, Ὅμηρος.

2. Sound Shifts, Accents, Iota Subscript & Punctuation

• οὐ / οὐκ / οὐχ ("not"): Uses οὐ before consonants (οὐ λύει); οὐκ before a smooth breathing (οὐκ ἔχει); οὐχ before a rough breathing (οὐχ ὁρᾷ).
• ἐκ / ἐξ & Elision (’): ἐκ before consonants, ἐξ before vowels (ἐξ ἀγροῦ). Short final vowels drop before vowels (ἀπ’ ἀγροῦ); rough breathings aspirate p/t/k → ph/th/kh (μετὰ ἡμέραν → μεθ’ ἡμέραν).
• Three Accents: Acute (ά), Grave (ὰ — replaces final acute mid-sentence), Circumflex (ᾶ). Distinguishes pairs like εἰμί (I am) vs εἶμι (I shall go), τίς; (who?) vs τις (someone).
• Iota Subscript (ᾳ, ῃ, ῳ): Silent iota written under long vowels — key marker of dative singular (τῇ χώρᾳ, τῷ λόγῳ) and words like σῴζω, ζῷον.
• Movable -ν & Punctuation: Optional -ν appears on -ε and -σι forms before vowels or sentence end (ἔλυσεν, ἐστίν, φύλαξιν). Question mark = ; (τίς εἶ;) | Colon/semicolon = raised dot · (high dot).

Prepositions by Case

+ Genitive: ἐκ / ἐξ (out of), ἀπό (from), πρό (before), ὑπό (by - agent), ἄνευ (without), πλήν (except)
+ Dative: ἐν (in, on), σύν (with), ἅμα (together with)
+ Accusative: εἰς (into), πρός (towards)
Variable cases: διά (+gen = through; +acc = because of), μετά (+gen = with; +acc = after), ἐπί (+gen/dat = on; +acc = against), παρά (+gen = from; +dat = beside; +acc = along/to)
